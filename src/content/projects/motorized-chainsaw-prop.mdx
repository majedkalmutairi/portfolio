## The pulley, the motor and the belt

The motor was a small problem to work around. Instead of a circular bore, I bought a motor with a D shaped bore. I looked up the product specifications online, and I modeled the pulley system around those measurements, making the bore hole around 2% smaller than the motor bore just to get a snug fit. It took a little bit of pressure to fit the bore into the hole of the pulley, but once it was in, it was stuck on.

The belt for the pulley system was another headache. I needed a material that was tough, resistant to heat from the constant friction, and strong enough to not stretch with time and lose its tension, thus breaking the whole pulley system. I landed on a sheet of leather I bought from Jarir. I got a big flat piece of it and cut it into thin lines, each about an inch in width. Putting 3 or 4 lines together would make a long enough belt for my pulley system, the problem was putting them together.

I first tried gluing, and made an entire belt just by gluing them together. It worked, but every time the glued parts went into the pulley system, it tugged on itself, and with enough rotations, it would break and not spin anymore. The reason was that the glue seeped into the cracks of the leather, making those parts hard and easy to get stuck in places that otherwise the leather would've just squeezed through.

So I stitched the lines of leather together, multiple lines together made another belt, and this time it worked! The belt spun endlessly. I left it running for about an hour, and came back to find it still spinning. Then I cut chainsaw teeth from HD foam and attached the teeth to the top of the leather, which wasn't porous like the bottom, meaning glue wouldn't seep through.
